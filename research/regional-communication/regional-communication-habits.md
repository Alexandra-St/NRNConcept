# Regional Communication Habits

## Goal

Define what can and cannot currently be concluded about messenger use across Narayana's potential markets.

## Evidence status

The previous version assigned a dominant set of messengers to broad regions without citing a comparable cross-country dataset. Those rankings have been removed. A platform's global scale, availability in a country or use in a particular community does not prove that it is the preferred channel for Narayana's target users.

No reliable public customer-geography data for Narayana was found in the [Communication Channels Audit](../communication-channels-audit.md). Regional channel decisions therefore require country-level evidence and internal customer data.

## Verified platform-level facts

- Telegram supports multiple accounts tied to different phone numbers in one app. Telegram also states that it has more than one billion active users globally. This does not establish country-level preference.
- WhatsApp documents substantial business-messaging activity in India and commerce features in Brazil. This supports market-specific business use, not a universal ranking across all user groups.
- Messaging ecosystems can be country-specific. A regional label such as “Southeast Asia” or “East Asia” is too broad to determine a single entry channel.

Sources: [Telegram FAQ](https://telegram.org/faq), [Business Messaging Ecosystem](../business-messaging-ecosystem.md).

## Claims not established by the current research

The available evidence does not establish that:

- WhatsApp is the default messenger throughout Western Europe;
- Telegram dominates Eastern Europe or the CIS as a whole;
- privacy-focused messengers have higher adoption in one broad region than another;
- WhatsApp is used mainly for international communication in North America;
- cryptocurrency adoption predicts demand for a telecom product;
- a messenger's national popularity predicts preference for support, purchase or account management in that messenger.

These may be research questions, but they should not be presented as findings.

## Required research design

For each priority country, collect comparable evidence for:

- monthly messenger use among the relevant age and customer segments;
- personal, business and support use as separate behaviours;
- Narayana acquisition source and support-channel usage;
- language and country distribution of active customers;
- willingness to purchase or manage telecom services in a third-party messenger;
- privacy and payment constraints by platform and country.

Use the same source period and population definition when comparing countries. Avoid combining app-download rankings, total registered accounts and survey-based monthly use as if they were the same metric.

## Current conclusion

Communication habits vary by country and segment, but this library does not yet contain enough comparable evidence to rank messengers by region. Regional channel choices remain hypotheses until supported by country-level data and Narayana's internal evidence.
