# User Journey and Entry Points: Research Framework

## Goal

Provide a consistent framework for investigating how people discover, evaluate, purchase and begin using privacy-focused telecom services.

## Evidence status

This document defines questions and candidate touchpoints. It does not describe an observed Narayana user journey. Channel preference, abandonment causes and the importance of each touchpoint require internal data or user research.

## 1. Trigger

Investigate the situation that starts the search, such as travel, relocation, work separation, privacy, local presence or a need for a specific communication capability.

Record the user's own language and the outcome they want. Do not assign a product category before understanding calls, SMS, data, country, duration and compatibility requirements.

## 2. Discovery

Candidate sources include search engines, recommendations, communities, video, social channels, comparison sites and developer resources.

Measure actual acquisition source and assisted conversions. An official channel or community mention does not establish that it is a significant discovery source.

## 3. Evaluation

Investigate which information is used to assess fit and trust:

- exact capabilities and exclusions;
- current price and availability;
- identity and registration requirements;
- privacy and data handling;
- compatibility limitations;
- documentation, reviews and independent discussion.

Separate company-provided trust signals from independent validation.

## 4. Purchase

Measure registration completion, payment success, time to fulfilment and failure reason. Record which personal information and confirmations are operationally or legally required.

## 5. First successful use

Define success per product: installed eSIM, working data session, assigned number, received supported SMS or completed call. “Time to first value” is a metric to measure, not an established finding in this research.

## 6. Support

Measure where users seek help, which questions can be resolved through documentation or automation, time to resolution, escalation rate and repeat contact. Do not infer preferred support channel from general messenger use.

## 7. Retention and exit

Investigate repeat purchase, renewal, continued use, cancellation and inactivity. Candidate causes include changed need, compatibility, reliability, price, support or setup effort; they remain hypotheses until coded from evidence.

## Minimum evidence

- anonymized acquisition and funnel data;
- support-ticket and refund-reason coding;
- interviews across successful, failed and abandoned journeys;
- country, language and product segmentation;
- observed task completion rather than stated preference alone.

## Current conclusion

The framework identifies what to measure at each stage. It does not yet establish the dominant entry points, the role of community, the most important trust signals or the main causes of abandonment for Narayana.
