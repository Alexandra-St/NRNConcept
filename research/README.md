# Research Library

## Purpose

This folder contains desk research and product hypotheses for Narayana. The documents are decision inputs, not proof of user demand.

## Evidence standard

Use the following labels consistently:

- **Verified fact** — supported by a cited source that directly substantiates the statement.
- **Company claim** — stated by the company being studied; useful for positioning analysis, but not independent validation.
- **Interpretation** — an analysis of verified material. It should not be presented as measured user behaviour.
- **Hypothesis** — a proposition that requires internal data, user interviews or an experiment.

Prefer first-party documentation for product capabilities and policies. Use independent research for market size, user behaviour, security quality and comparative performance. A company's own website can verify what it says and offers, but not that its claims are objectively true.

## Writing standard

- Use calm, neutral English.
- Prefer specific statements to slogans and superlatives.
- Name the population, geography and date when describing behaviour.
- Do not generalize from platform availability to user preference.
- Do not generalize from a use case to its prevalence.
- Use **may**, **could** or **appears** when evidence is incomplete.
- Keep product implications under a dedicated **Implications for Narayana** or **Hypotheses to test** heading.
- Record the research date for time-sensitive audits.

## Current limitations

The library relies mainly on public desk research. It does not currently contain representative Narayana customer interviews, product analytics, acquisition data or a cross-market messenger-usage dataset. Claims about Narayana users, conversion, preferred channels and regional demand therefore remain hypotheses unless explicitly linked to internal evidence.
