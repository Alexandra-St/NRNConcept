# Competitor: SimpleX

## Purpose

Assess how SimpleX explains a messaging architecture without global user identifiers.

## Evidence status

Review date: 18 July 2026.

Architecture and feature statements are based on SimpleX's official documentation. Communication conclusions are interpretations. Comparative privacy and usability claims were not independently tested.

## Verified facts and company claims

SimpleX states that its network does not assign global identifiers to users. Contacts are established through invitation links or QR codes rather than by searching a permanent user address.

The project publishes source code, protocol documentation and developer materials. It describes the absence of a global identifier as a way to reduce correlation across conversations.

## Communication interpretation

SimpleX explains privacy primarily through architecture. The central idea is repeated consistently: reducing the identity data created by the network can be preferable to collecting and then protecting it.

## Strengths observed in the communication

- A specific, technically distinguishable proposition.
- Public documentation explains how contacts are established.
- The product claim is connected to inspectable architecture and source code.

## Limitations of this review

- No independent protocol assessment was conducted.
- The review does not establish superiority over other messengers.
- Claims about learning curve, mainstream appeal or user preference require research with users.

## Implications for Narayana

**Interpretation:** Narayana should distinguish between collecting less identity data and protecting identity data after collection.

**Hypothesis to test:** users will better understand Narayana's privacy model when the product explains what information is never requested, what is optional and what is operationally required.
