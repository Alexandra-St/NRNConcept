# Competitor: Session

## Purpose

Assess how Session positions a messenger that does not require a phone number or email address at signup.

## Evidence status

Review date: 18 July 2026.

Architecture and feature statements are based on Session's official site and documentation. Communication conclusions are interpretations. This review does not independently validate security, decentralization or comparative metadata protection.

## Verified facts and company claims

Session describes itself as an end-to-end encrypted, decentralized messenger. It uses account IDs and does not require a phone number or email address to sign up.

Session states that it uses onion-routed paths and publishes its source code. These are company claims about the system; a technical comparison would require protocol review and independent evidence.

## Communication interpretation

Session leads with the absence of phone-number registration and with metadata-related architecture. The public site uses short benefit statements, while the documentation provides the technical detail.

## Strengths observed in the communication

- A clear differentiator: no phone number or email at signup.
- Technical mechanisms are paired with short explanations of their intended benefit.
- Source code and documentation are publicly accessible.

## Limitations of this review

- No onboarding or reliability testing was conducted.
- The review cannot support claims such as “maximum metadata protection”.
- It does not establish the size or composition of Session's audience.

## Implications for Narayana

**Interpretation:** removing an identifier is a different privacy proposition from hiding or encrypting it.

**Hypothesis to test:** some Narayana users value identity separation more than account discoverability, but the size and needs of this segment are unknown.
