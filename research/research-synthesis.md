# Research Synthesis

## Goal

Summarize what the current desk research supports, what it suggests and what still requires validation.

## Supported findings

### 1. Phone numbers can have several roles

Official platform documentation shows that a phone number can be a communication endpoint, an account-registration input and a discovery mechanism. The role differs by service. Signal, for example, still requires a number to register but provides controls that limit disclosure and discoverability.

### 2. Telecom product categories are not interchangeable

Travel eSIMs, mobile subscriptions, virtual numbers and SIP services can provide different combinations of data, calls, SMS, country codes and persistence. Compatibility must be verified for the selected product and external service.

### 3. Reviewed privacy companies keep core transactions in first-party channels

The channel audit found that websites and first-party apps are the main purchase and product interfaces in the reviewed sample. Support typically uses documentation, email, forms and ticketing. No reviewed company was verified as using a Telegram or WhatsApp bot as the primary end-to-end telecom management interface.

### 4. Telegram and WhatsApp support business workflows

Official platform materials verify bots, Mini Apps, business conversations and market-specific commerce or automation features. This proves technical and operational precedent, not Narayana user demand or a preferred platform.

### 5. Telecom assistants can resolve real service tasks

Vodafone and Deutsche Telekom document assistants used at material scale for support and account-related journeys. These examples support feasibility but do not establish that a third-party messenger should become Narayana's primary interface.

### 6. Competitors use different trust mechanisms

The reviewed companies publish combinations of open-source code, audits, governance information, data policies, product limitations and technical documentation. These are inspectable trust signals. Their effect on user trust or conversion was not measured here.

## Interpretations

- Scenario-led explanations may help users distinguish products with similar marketing labels but different capabilities.
- Data minimization, explicit limitations and clear compatibility boundaries may be especially important for a privacy-oriented telecom service.
- A conversational assistant may add value in product clarification and support when connected to authoritative catalog, account and service-status data.

These are reasoned interpretations, not validated outcomes.

## Unresolved hypotheses

The research does not yet establish:

- which problems are most common among Narayana customers;
- which countries or languages account for most demand;
- which messengers those customers use or prefer for each task;
- how users currently discover and evaluate Narayana;
- whether scenario-led navigation improves conversion or selection accuracy;
- whether identity separation is a primary motivation for additional-number purchases;
- whether an AI or messenger assistant improves resolution, conversion or retention;
- which registration, payment, activation or compatibility issues cause abandonment.

## Recommended next evidence

1. Analyse anonymized acquisition, product, country, support and refund data.
2. Interview customers with successful, failed and abandoned journeys.
3. Code recurring use cases and compatibility requirements.
4. Test scenario-led product selection against the current experience.
5. Pilot a narrow assistant workflow with authoritative data and measure completion, escalation and error rates.

## Conclusion

The current research supports a product direction centred on clearer explanations, explicit constraints and evidence-backed trust signals. It does not yet support broad claims about user behaviour, regional channel dominance or demand for a messenger-first product. Those decisions require Narayana-specific evidence.
