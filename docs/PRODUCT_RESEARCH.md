# Product Research — Narayana

Independent NRN concept research/design notes. References to Narayana describe public research context and hypotheses, not commissioning, approval or production integration. Planning statements are not implementation evidence; see the [repository README](../README.md).

## 1. What does Narayana sell?

At first glance, Narayana sells:

- virtual phone numbers;
- eSIM;
- physical SIM cards;
- SIP telephony;
- privacy-oriented telecom services.

However, these are products, not the value itself.

### Product conclusion

Narayana does not primarily sell telecom services.

It sells:

- privacy;
- control;
- flexibility;
- freedom of communication.

The telecom products are simply tools to achieve these goals.

---

## 2. Target audience

### Primary audience

Ordinary people who:

- care about privacy;
- travel;
- work remotely;
- need additional phone numbers;
- don't fully understand digital privacy but want to be safer online.

### Secondary audience

- Digital nomads.
- Small businesses.
- IT professionals.
- Privacy enthusiasts.

---

## 3. User pains

The user doesn't buy "a virtual number".

They buy a solution to a problem.

Examples:

- I don't want to give my personal phone number to every website.
- I want to separate work and personal communication.
- I travel often and need connectivity abroad.
- I don't know who has access to my personal information.
- I want more control over my communications.
- I want to reduce my digital footprint.

---

## 4. Why choose Narayana instead of a traditional telecom operator?

Traditional operators sell connectivity.

Narayana sells communication with additional properties.

Examples:

- more privacy;
- greater control;
- international flexibility;
- less dependence on local telecom ecosystems.

The user changes not just the provider, but the approach to communication.

---

## 5. Product differentiation

Compared to traditional operators, Narayana focuses on:

- privacy-first philosophy;
- international communication;
- virtual identities (additional numbers);
- flexible telecom services.

The differentiation is not price.

The differentiation is values.

---

## 6. Brand values

The product communicates several recurring values:

- Privacy.
- Freedom.
- Control.
- Independence.
- Simplicity.

---

## 7. Tone of Voice

Current communication style:

- technical;
- engineering-oriented;
- calm;
- professional;
- minimalistic.

### Observation

Many product benefits are described through technologies rather than user problems.

Example:

Instead of answering

> "Can somebody listen to my calls?"

the website explains

> "TLS/SRTP."

---

## 8. Strengths

Current strengths:

- strong philosophy;
- coherent positioning;
- consistent branding;
- unique niche;
- clean interface;
- interesting product ecosystem.

---

## 9. Weaknesses / Opportunities

Biggest opportunity:

Translate technical advantages into understandable human benefits.

Current communication often explains:

- how the technology works,

instead of

- why the user should care.

This creates an opportunity for:

- interactive education;
- visual explanations;
- user scenarios;
- privacy simulators.

---

## 10. User Triggers

Events that may lead someone to search for Narayana:

- moving abroad;
- becoming a digital nomad;
- starting a business;
- wanting a second phone number;
- concerns about privacy;
- data leaks;
- online harassment;
- frequent travelling;
- separating work and personal life.

---

## Product hypothesis

Narayana communicates primarily in the language of engineers.

Our opportunity is to become a bridge between engineering and ordinary users by translating technical capabilities into understandable everyday value.

---

## Final conclusion

Narayana's biggest competitive advantage is not its telecom products.

It is the philosophy behind them.

The biggest opportunity is to help ordinary people understand why that philosophy matters.
